name: Savoir source attribution
description: Citation conventions for the French Savoir plant fiches and social-media context
---

Use the exact bibliographic title recorded in the research source registry for each scientific link shown in the app. Keep each note limited to what the cited source actually supports. Social posts may document reported practices, but must remain labeled as testimony and must not be treated as medical evidence or botanical identification. Culinary pages support preparation details, not therapeutic claims. A recipe's repetition across sites or a small online rating is only a limited popularity signal, not proof of efficacy, safety, or representative approval.

**Why:** Shortened or invented citation titles make it harder for readers to verify a claim, while social snippets, small self-selected ratings, and lab studies can be overinterpreted as proof of local food practice or health benefit.

**How to apply:** When adding or editing Savoir fiches or articles, reconcile link titles and claim wording against `research/sources.json`; include a direct regional culinary source for recipe instructions, label social material as testimony, and separate food or sensory evidence from clinical evidence.
